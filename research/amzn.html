Thesis

I have started buying Amazon at $97.25 due to the recent bear market, where the company's stock dropped significantly after a twenty to one share split. The whole technology market saw a decrease due to the sell-off of growth stocks that were overvalued, but I think that the market overlooks Amazon's significant presence in the cloud space and its most profitable segment, AWS. The company's retail segment was growing rapidly before pandemic restrictions, but the growth was artificial and caused by panic buying and people having more leisure time to buy non-necessity goods. After the pandemic, Amazon had to close the warehouses it opened to meet demand, so I believe that now the retail segment will stop growing rapidly, not because of negative trends but because the company deliberately reduces its footprint in it. This conclusion is based on calculations: the shares are priced at 1.9 times sales, and the company's value is about $992 billion, which is a relatively low price for such a giant, despite a significant one-time write-down of the investment in Rivian and heavy R&D spending. Sales growth is close to 10% despite overall decline in consumer spending, and the operating income is positive despite one-time expenses, which suggests that the company has an excellent income-generating base that delivers a stable profit. Its value is about 18.6 times EBITDA, which is also a fair price. The last statistic that attracted me was the company's debt: $54.3 billion in cash against $67.2 billion, which is not a high number given the scale of Amazon's operations. I believe that the discrepancy between the high value of the company and the income from operations is what will bring good returns in the future. My initial investment in the company is justified by the fact that AWS dominates the cloud computing market and is expected to grow in the future due to its adoption by various organizations. Advertising, which I did not consider before investing, also became a profitable segment for the company. Finally, the profit from retail sales increased more rapidly than I expected due to significant warehouse closures and optimization of costs. Now, instead of a loss, the company has a stable profit margin across all segments of its work. In the future, I will monitor the expenditures on cloud technologies, including artificial intelligence, to ensure that they are not too high in the short term because such a move might eliminate the possibility of profit growth in the near future. Thus, Amazon is my favorite stock to invest in the long term, and I do not plan to sell it in the near future.
