Thesis

I am buying Bank of America at $41.96 on this selloff, as bank stocks broadly slide on fears that higher interest rates will hurt bond holdings and slow the growth in lending. Regional and big banks alike get caught in the same fear, but BofA has the biggest and stickiest customer deposit base of any major U.S. bank, cultivated through its large branch network and consumer banking business. So I think the selloff is just a short-term risk factor, and the numbers support this idea. I am buying at approximately 13 times earnings on a bank that is turning over more than a 9 percent return on equity, which is a decent price for an average bank and cheap for a dominant one. The market cap of around $330 billion for the company, stretched out across nearly 8 billion shares, is more than a safe bet for the most important bank in the country. Higher rates, the same risk factor that is frightening everyone these days, will eventually lead to more interest income once the fears around losses on bond holdings die down. I continue to hold this because the interest income has grown as rates played out exactly as I had expected, and Bank of America has been quietly buying back a tremendous amount of its stock, growing my share of the company without me having to do a thing. The deposit base that convinced me to buy has held up through more than one round of bank-sector stress since then. I do not expect this stock to have much of a move, and that's the whole point. It is meant to grow steadily in the background, supported by a size advantage that smaller banks cannot begin to match, and I plan to hold it as a long-term core position instead of trading around every rate headline.
