Thesis

I am buying Citi stock at $50.36 as a value and turnaround play. The stock trades at a significant discount to the bank's own books, and offers the best value among the big US banks in my opinion. Years of difficult and dysfunctional international diversification and large expenses have taken their toll. The new CEO Jane Fraser has started a turnaround plan, selling off non-core businesses and focusing on cutting costs across the board, and I want to buy now ahead of this new "era." The numbers support this view. At 0.59 times tangible book value, the stock is trading below what the bank believes its business is worth. With 1.9 billion shares outstanding, the company is worth about $95.7 billion at the current price. At 12.5x earnings and a mid-single-digit return on equity, there appears to be limited downside based on its current value. At the same time, there is room for profitability and cost-cutting to improve, which could give the stock significant upside if the strategy works. I believe my decision to buy the stock is supported by both its value and its long-term potential. The turnaround is making progress, as Citi continues to reduce its exposure to non-core international retail banking, cut costs, and improve profitability. The stock also remains attractively valued compared to its peers, even though it has risen since I first bought it. The large discount to tangible book value that originally attracted me is still there, although it has become smaller. I am investing based on a long-term view and am willing to wait while the bank continues its restructuring and cost-cutting efforts over the next few years. Turnarounds are rarely smooth, so I'm aware the stock will likely remain somewhat volatile as Citi works to improve earnings and control costs. However, I believe the company's fundamentals can continue to improve over time.
