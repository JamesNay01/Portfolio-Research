Thesis

I am buying GE at $105.40 while the company splits into three separate businesses: GE Aerospace, GE Vernova, and GE HealthCare. What I really want is the aerospace side, GE Aerospace, since jet engines make most of their money years after they're sold, through decades of repairs, maintenance, and spare parts and not the original sale. I believe splitting this business from the rest of the old industrial company, which has struggled for years with underperforming divisions and heavy pension costs, will help management to focus and actually run it effectively. The numbers I have access to are messy because of the split, but they still support my idea. The aerospace business is growing revenue more than 20 percent a year, with operating margins of around 20 percent, helped by more people flying again and a growing number of engines out there needing service. GE has more cash than debt heading into the split, about $17.3 billion in cash against $18.4 billion in debt at the company level, so the new standalone business will not start out buried in debt like some past GE restructurings. I have held on to GE Aerospace because the plan worked out how I hoped, a more focused management team, a cleaner balance sheet, and a business that is benefiting directly from more people flying around the world. The distraction of running an unrelated healthcare and power business along with aerospace is gone. With so many LEAP and other engines already out there needing years of future maintenance work, the steady repair-and-parts income I was counting on when I bought is now showing up clearly in the segment's own numbers instead of being buried inside a bigger company. I don't see much reason to sell this one early and think it's a position I want to hold long-term barring any major developments in the sector.
