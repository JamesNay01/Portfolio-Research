Thesis

I am buying Eli Lilly at $359.17 betting on the company's GLP-1 drugs to extend significantly beyond diabetes into the weight loss space. The value of the data supporting tirzepatide, sold as Mounjaro for the treatment of diabetes, is better than the competition, and I think the company is in a good spot to create one of the largest pharmaceutical franchises in the industry if obesity therapy and insurance coverage take place in the way I hope for the next several years. I understand that I am paying a premium. Shares were trading at around 52 times earnings and over 11 times sales, a price that only makes sense if the growth idea really takes off and not based on what the company earns now. What makes me feel confident enough to pay this price is a return on equity near 50 percent and gross margins over 75 percent, indicators that this was already a very well-run, efficient business before the opportunity in weight loss really took off, supported by a market cap nearly $323 billion and fairly manageable debt of $18.7 billion. I still hold this, and it is one of my best performing positions in the entire portfolio. Mounjaro and its weight-loss version Zepbound have exceeded my initial expectations, insurance coverage has grown faster than I anticipated and Lilly's pipeline of drugs behind these products tells me this is not a one-drug story and rather a shift in production and what the company can do. Valuation is my primary concern ahead, since a lot of future growth is already wrapped up in the stock price, but given how massive the market for obesity and metabolic disease treatment could be worldwide, I am comfortable holding on through the ups and downs rather than trying to time an exit around any one data release or competitor announcement.
