Thesis

I'm buying NextEra Energy at $78.69 to diversify my portfolio into a reliable income-producing asset that has less volatility than some of my other higher-growth stocks. NextEra is the largest utility company by market value and, to a lesser extent, the largest wind and solar power producer in the world. So I'm getting the dual benefit of a stable regulated utility with a generous dividend, and an economic moat in the renewable energy sector, which I think will see significant growth in the decades to come. NextEra's financials support the fair valuation, as a utility with a P/E ratio of around 27 on adjusted earnings is still quite expensive. But an 11% return on equity and consistently high-teens operating margins are befitting of a power producer with a large installed base of electricity generation and distribution infrastructure to tap into. The balance sheet is levered, with around $74.4 billion in debt against under $1 billion in cash, which is entirely reasonable for a capital-intensive utility that requires constant investment in power generation facilities and electric grid infrastructure to continue its operations. The cash flow from operations is sufficient to fund the capital expenditures required to maintain the growth trajectory. I'm buying NextEra Energy because it does exactly what I want as an investor: it provides reliable but unexciting returns through steady growth without having to think about it too much. The next generation of renewables is providing a nice tailwind for the stock, as constructing wind and solar farms is a profitable field for NextEra to occupy in a regulated monopoly setting. The increase in electricity consumption from data centers and the transition to electric vehicles and heat pumps will become a tailwind for the utility that I'm quite excited about, and it was a factor I hadn't considered when I made the decision to buy the stock. It will continue to be one of the highest-yielding and most dependable stocks in my portfolio in years to come.
