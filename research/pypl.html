Thesis

I am buying PayPal at $79.17 on the belief that the market overreacted to the soft growth and increased competition. PayPal is one of the largest online payment processors with a strong brand name and wide merchant acceptance, and Venmo has significant monetization potential that the company has not yet fully realized given its size. The price supports my thesis, as I am buying under four times sales and twenty times non-GAAP earnings for a company that is still growing its revenue at a high single-digit rate, generating meaningful free cash flow, and has a balance sheet worth over $10 billion in cash against about the same amount of debt. With around 1.14 billion shares outstanding and a market cap near $90.4 billion, this is not the sign of a value trap but rather a lack of interest from the market due to fears of competition. I am still long the stock, although I must admit it has not gone the way I wanted, and it is currently under my entry cost, which I think is better said plainly rather than in euphemisms. Competition in checkout, particularly with Apple Pay and buy-now-pay-later firms, has been more challenging than I expected, and maintaining branded checkout share has proven more difficult than I had anticipated. What has kept me long is the shift in focus from volume to profitability and quality growth under new leadership, and the price looks more reasonable now given the risk-reward situation, as much of the competition concern is already priced in. I am watching this closely and will reevaluate if things do not continue to improve.
