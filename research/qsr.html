Thesis

I am buying Restaurant Brands International at $66.67 for its combination of steady cash flow and a turnaround in the works at Burger King. As a company with three big brands, Burger King, Tim Hortons, and Popeyes, I am not putting all my eggs in one basket. Tim Hortons remains a dominant Canadian brand with loyal customers, Popeyes is growing its presence internationally, and Burger King's plan to renew its restaurants and update its kitchens gives me confidence that years of neglect will finally be overcome. The numbers support a fair price. Traded at around 22 times adjusted earnings and 3 times sales with a market value of nearly $20.1 billion, Restaurant Brands International is not a steal, but operating margins in the mid-30s thanks to the franchise model and revenue growth of nearly 14 percent this year show a company that is making good money without much of its own investment. In addition to a nice dividend while waiting for the turnaround at Burger King to really take off in the sales numbers, I am getting a reliable cash flow from the company. I am continuing to hold this investment because the turnaround has taken on a slow and steady pace rather than a spectacular leap that would exceed my expectations for a restaurant remodeling program. I am not looking for a fast-growing stock and do not expect one, but the cash flow supports the dividend and allows for continued reinvestment in the Burger King fix. I think of this as a lower-risk, income-producing alternative to higher-growth peers in my portfolio, and am quite happy to hold out for slow progress in the turnaround process as long as the sales trends continue to move in the right direction.
