Thesis

I am buying Charles Schwab at $94.94 due to the 2023 regional bank crisis, where concerns about banks' losses on their bond portfolios dragged down Schwab's price despite its fundamental difference from a typical banking institution. Schwab runs the largest brokerage and custodial platform in the country with tens of billions of dollars in client funds, and I believe that the current selloff is a temporary balance-sheet scare that hides the true value of Schwab's extremely loyal customer base that takes decades to build. At the price I'm buying, the stock is at approximately 32.6 times earnings, which suggests the market has already priced in the worst-case scenario of client deposits being withdrawn and the business's fundamentals being re-discounted, and it is now beginning to reflect the value of the company's underlying health. Net margins well above 30 percent and a mid-teens return on equity suggest that the company's core operations remained healthy throughout the period of turmoil; the stock price is simply catching up to that reality. I expect to stay long given that is precisely the scenario that played out. The deposit pressures that caused the 2023 panic have eased off as expected once the panic subsided, and the combination with TD Ameritrade, which preceded my purchase, has continued to deliver cost savings and additional business from cross-selling to shared customers. I believe the 2023 panic provided a better entry point than justified by the fundamentals, and Schwab's dominant position in the wealth management and custody space is difficult to overcome by smaller regional competitors. I expect the position to continue to benefit from the merger synergies being realized over the coming years.
