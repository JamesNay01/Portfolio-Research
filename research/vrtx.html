Thesis

I'm buying Vertex Pharmaceuticals at $369.50 because it has a virtual monopoly on the cure for cystic fibrosis. There is no competitive product which acts on the underlying genetics rather than the symptoms, which provides Vertex with significant pricing power and a long runway before patents begin to expire and hurt the business. I want exposure to biotech without the potentially fatal risk of relying on a pipeline that has not yet delivered its goods, because while the main drug is approved and doing well, there is not that much risk involved. Profits are unusually large for a biotech, with Vertex enjoying a 37 percent net margin and a 91 percent gross margin, figures that are typically only seen in drug companies that do not spend the money on research and development that Vertex spends, with an investment of approximately $10.7 billion in combined cash and investments and almost no debt. At about 29 times earnings for that level of quality, funded by a drug with basically no real competition, and a market cap near $97.5 billion, the fair price estimate given the typical profit rates for comparable growth is about right, with Vertex's cash flow allowing it to fund research into other diseases like non-opioid pain treatment and Type 1 diabetes without needing to turn to external funding or dilution to continue its research into mid-stage trials. I'm buying this as it provides the security of a profitable core business that typically pays large amounts of cash like a royalty on the cure for cystic fibrosis, while also providing the upside potential of a drug that can come out of its pipeline. It's one of the most balanced positions among the higher growth stocks in my portfolio.
