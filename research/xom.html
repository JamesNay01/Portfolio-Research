Thesis

I am buying ExxonMobil at $118.05 on the belief that they've become far more conservative in spending. Following years of wasteful spending on growth projects that the market rightfully punished the stock for, Exxon has evidently changed their approach to spending, directing growth capital towards their cheapest oil to produce, particularly the Permian Basin in Texas and offshore Guyana, as opposed to the lower-yielding projects which used to define the company's reputation for extravagant waste during the last oil cycle. The price reflects the market's belief that this won't last. I'm paying less than 9x earnings and approximately 9x free cash flow, with a market cap near $497 billion, for a company which just grew earnings more than 150 percent year-over-year fueled by high energy prices. Despite the stock's substantial rebound from its lows, the market is still pricing Exxon as if the stronger results and judicious spending won't be sustained, allowing me a healthy cushion, bolstered further by $29.6 billion in cash against $41.3 billion in debt. I still hold this because Guyana has grown far faster and more profitably than I anticipated upon purchase, with each new phase coming online roughly on schedule, while management has maintained the judicious spending I was expecting as opposed to reverting to the spend-at-any-cost approach of the last oil cycle under previous leadership. The dividend and stock buybacks together provide me a tangible cash return as a shareholder while Guyana's next phases ramp up, and I view the refining and chemicals side of the business as a useful partial hedge against the volatility of oil prices that a pure oil-drilling company would not offer. The bump several months ago from the US intervention also provided a nice boost to the stock, which I believe will continue.
