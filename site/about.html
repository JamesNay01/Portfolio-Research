About

I'm James, a Freshman at Wake Forest University with a long-running interest in business and investing. I started this portfolio in October 2022 on Investopedia's trading simulator to build real research and decision-making experience without capital risk, still sizing positions deliberately, writing down my thesis in a stock before buying, and holding through volatility rather than trading the headlines. This site is where I track that portfolio and publish the reasoning behind it, including the calls that haven't worked, along with those that have.

Investment Philosophy

Reading back across all fourteen positions, the same handful of judgment calls show up again and again. Here's the pattern, with the positions that actually demonstrate each one.

Paying for good companies

When a business has a durable, specific competitive advantage, I'm willing to pay a higher multiple for it rather than wait for a discount that may never come.

NVDACDNSVRTX

Buy value when the fear is temporary and sector-wide

My best value plays share a pattern: the market panicking about something macro or sector-wide rather than a company-specific problem that's actually getting fixed.

CXOMSCHW

Hold through volatility instead of trading the news

Almost every position states explicitly that I'm holding through short-term moves rather than trying to time entries and exits around quarterly results or headlines.

BACQSR

Size positions for how much uncertainty I'm expecting

Low-volatility compounders are used as a stable part of the portfolio, while positions with a wider range of possible outcomes are sized and reviewed more carefully.

NEETSLA

Say plainly when a thesis isn't working

A research site is only useful if the losing calls are as visible as the winners. Nobody wins every trade, so learning from the losers is just as important as learning from the winners.
